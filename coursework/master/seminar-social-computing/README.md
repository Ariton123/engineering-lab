# Ethical Considerations in Social Computing — Seminar Project

## Overview

This repository contains a seminar project developed for the **Seminar Social Computing** course during the master's program in Computer Science.

The project explores ethical considerations in social computing, with a specific focus on privacy, transparency, fair treatment, and discrimination risks in shared workforce arrangements. The final report connects social computing ethics with the **Balanced Workforce** concept, where employees may be temporarily loaned or shared between companies.

## Project Context

Social computing technologies shape how people communicate, collaborate, and participate in digital environments. In workforce management contexts, these systems can introduce ethical challenges related to employee treatment, privacy, transparency, and decision-making.

This project focuses on the ethical risks faced by loaned employees, including:

* unequal treatment compared to permanent staff
* job security concerns
* privacy risks from intercompany data sharing
* opaque decision-making and unclear loan agreement policies
* lack of employee participation in decisions affecting their role

## Main Contribution

The project proposes an **Ethical Balanced Workforce Framework** for supporting fairer and more transparent shared workforce arrangements.

The framework focuses on:

* equity and fairness
* transparency
* privacy protection
* participatory design
* intercompany collaboration
* fair treatment policies
* privacy safeguards
* surveys and interviews
* case study analysis
* ethical guideline development

## Report Summary

The final report discusses ethical issues in shared workforce systems and proposes a decision-making framework for reducing discrimination and improving fairness for loaned employees.

It covers:

* background of social computing
* ethical challenges in workforce-sharing systems
* discrimination against loaned employees
* job security concerns
* privacy and data handling risks
* transparency challenges
* ethical decision-making principles
* implementation guidelines
* survey-based evaluation
* SWOT analysis
* leadership development and training ideas
